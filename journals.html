A Handmade Web....

I think it's interesting to consider the web being used as a space that can transcend physical deterioration and time, unlike many other media. Functioning as an archive for physical works, the web even has the capacity to outlast its original physical copy. It's also interesting to think about the ways in which physical things can be translated into digital spaces. I've never considered that the reader's ability to view the source of a web page functioned as an act of co-authorship. The visitor of a webpage doesn't have to be a passive participant, they have the ability to edit and access the code and can thus have a hand in the authorship of a page.

My Website is a Shifting House....

I appreciate the author's poetic use of metaphors to explain the use and purpose of websites. She relates them to puddles, rooms, gardens, a thrown rock. I particularly enjoyed the metaphor she used about the shelf. It is a small fragment of an entire room, and can only hold so much weight. It can be rearranged and composed of randomly juxtaposed items that simply need a space to exist. A website doesn't have to be a whole complex structure, it can be a small, simple space that is meant to be disorganized and constantly rearranged.

A Rant on Technology....

In such a technologically advanced age, it is easy to overlook the simple technologies that we rely on everyday. When I think of the word "technology," it's true that the first things that come to mind are advanced structures like smartphones, computers, and planes. Even the delineation between the terms "hi-tech" and "low-tech" seems to cheapen the value of these important technologies we've relied on for thousands of years. Everyday utencils such as knives, scissors, pencils, paper, books, even clothes. There is much less respect and appreciation given to these forms of technology which are equally, if not more important than newer, hi-tech gadgets.

Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web....

It's disturbing to think of how hostile the web has become to its users over the years. Growing up on the internet, I remember it being a much more creative and community-based space. Aimlessly clicking around and looking up my favorite cartoons eventually lead me to sites like DeviantArt, random fanmade forums, passion sites, etc. These days its so much more predatory and stumbling across these types of websites is a lot harder, you actually have to dig. I remember one of my favorite things to do on the computer when I was younger was to look up pictures of different characters I liked and scroll through all the fanart. I loved to see all the different styles and interpretations, and I found so many cool artists that way. Now google has become polluted with so much AI, it doesn't even feel like the web is a place for humans anymore.

The Internet's Back-to-the-Land Movement....

The idea of a "low-tech" web sounds really appealing to me, I would love to see the web restored to the state it once was when I was kid. I think this would be incredibly useful for younger and older generations, who are especially vulnerable to the web's predatory nature. It could be a space where the youth could explore the accessibility, creativity, and possibilities of the web without worry that they are being exploited. I know the current web also targets the elderly who have significantly less knowledge or fluency of the internet, by stealing their information/baiting them into spending money...this would be a great solution for that issue and would make being online fun for everyone again.
